I think we're getting to the level below "visual style" now—you're starting to define the **emotional vocabulary** of the brand. That's where memorable design comes from.

A few thoughts as this evolves:

### Presence through absence becomes "evidence"

I actually think "presence through absence" can be refined into **Evidence of Love**.

Not symbols of death.
Not symbols of babies.

Evidence that someone existed.

Examples:
- Tiny footprints disappearing into wet sand.
- A single candle still burning after everyone has left.
- An indentation in a pillow.
- A swing moving gently with nobody there (perhaps too child-specific).
- Ripples across still water.
- A rainbow appearing in otherwise ordinary weather.
- Morning light catching dust in the air.

The image says:
> Someone mattered.

---

### Leave a mark

The quilt is fascinating because it's participatory.

The pattern isn't remembrance.

It's **making sure they continue to exist in the world.**

That's a much stronger concept.

Every stitch.
Every footprint.
Every handwritten name.
Every candle.

They're acts of saying

> "My baby existed."

That aligns perfectly with

> *A little life, not a little loss.*

---

### The website itself could behave this way

This is where it gets interesting.

Not literally, but philosophically.

Small traces.

Soft highlights where you've been.

Gentle transitions.

Nothing suddenly appears or disappears.

The interface leaves a memory of interaction.

Very subtle, but it reinforces the idea.

---

### Light works best as photography

I agree.

I'd avoid trying to make "light" into decorative UI.

Use it in photography.

- window light
- sunrise
- candlelight
- silhouettes
- translucent curtains

Then let the interface stay restrained.

---

### Recovery paths become structure

I don't think they need to be illustrated.

Instead they become invisible alignment.

Cards connected.

Sections gently offset.

Eye movement naturally flowing.

You feel guided without noticing why.

---

### Quiet discovery

I think this becomes one of the site's defining UX principles.

Nothing should ever feel like

> "Contact us now."

Everything feels like

> "Start wherever feels comfortable."

That's a very different interaction model.

---

### "We remember too."

This might actually become a recurring phrase.

Imagine it appearing in little places.

Above testimonials.

Near remembrance events.

On the About page.

Not as branding, but as reassurance.

---

## One thought I'd add

Reading the IA again, one thing stands out.

The site isn't really about grief.

It's about **continuing the relationship**.

Most bereavement sites implicitly help people let go.

Sands, from everything you've described and from the content schedule, seems to acknowledge that parents often don't want to let go. They want their baby to continue to matter.

That's an important distinction.

The design shouldn't communicate closure.

It should communicate **continuity**.

Not "moving on."

Not "healing."

But

> "Your baby's place in your life is permanent."

That single shift changes almost every design decision. It makes the site feel like somewhere that understands parents rather than somewhere trying to fix them.
