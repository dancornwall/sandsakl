# Page 3 — Orientation: "When your baby dies"

**Status:** draft, for review. **Copy on this page still requires dual mother + bereaved-father review before ship** (content schedule's highest-priority flag) — this brief specs structure only, so review can happen once copy is drafted against this shape.
**Template:** standard (Page 0).
**URL:** `orientation/my-baby-died`.
**Entry points:** (a) Home hero CTA, direct — the majority-case first-time visitor lands here without seeing the Orientation index at all; (b) Orientation index, row 1.
**Job of this page:** the single highest-stakes page in the set. Meet someone in acute, early grief — any relationship to the baby — without making them self-sort into a category before they feel recognised. Hand off to concrete next actions once they're ready, not before.

---

## A. Structural decision — hybrid body (see decision log)

*Three shapes were considered: (1) fully unified body, no visible split — relies entirely on careful "you"-language to avoid mother-default; (2) fully split into three explicit lanes (mother / father-partner / solo-parent) — risks feeling like a form, and risks the "forces an unwanted grief comparison" problem the content schedule warns about elsewhere; (3) hybrid — one continuous, unranked body with explicit inclusion woven in, plus an in-page anchor nav so no one has to read the whole page to know they're held here. **Hybrid chosen.***

**What "hybrid" means structurally, so it isn't quietly rebuilt as option 2:**
- This is **one continuous page**, not boxed/titled sub-sections ("For Mothers" / "For Fathers" as separate visual containers). The anchors below jump to moments *within* the same flowing body copy.
- The anchor list itself must not imply ranking by order. Keep it short, equal weight, and include a general/any-relationship anchor alongside the named ones — not just a mother/father/solo-parent list, which would itself read as "pick your category."

## C. In-page anchor nav

*New addition (Dan) — addresses skim-reading in crisis: someone scanning quickly (a father, most likely, per the risk this solves) needs to see he's addressed here without reading the full page top to bottom.*

**Revised approach (see decision log):** the first draft of this section tried to solve "don't rank mother/father" by *not naming either explicitly* — a "General / whoever you are" anchor standing in for both. That was wrong, and worth stating why: unmarked grief language defaults to maternal (the design system says so directly — "my baby died" defaults to mother), so an anchor set that avoids naming isn't neutral, it's invisible inclusion — which reads to a father as absence, not welcome. Naming and ranking are different problems. The fix is to **name everyone, equally weighted** — not to avoid naming anyone.

- A short row of anchor links, equal visual weight — same size, same styling, no visual first-place. No numbering.
- **Anchor set:** **Mothers · Fathers & partners · On your own** (labels tbc — see open questions), each pointing to a moment in the body where that reader is explicitly, plainly addressed. A fourth, general anchor may sit alongside these for anyone who doesn't see themselves in the other three — but it does not replace or stand in for naming mother/father directly.
- Placement: directly below the intro block (B), above the main body (D). Sits quietly — not styled as a second navigation bar competing with the header nav.
- Each anchor scrolls to a point *within* the continuous body copy below (section D) — not to a separate boxed subsection. The page stays one continuous piece; the anchors are wayfinding into it, not a table of contents for separate lanes.

## Decision log
- **Hybrid structure chosen** over fully-unified or fully-split, per discussion above (section A).
- **In-page anchor nav added** (Dan's addition, not in original content schedule) — specifically to solve skim-reading risk in an acute-crisis read, most likely to affect fathers/partners who may disengage before finding themselves in flowing prose.
- **Anchor naming reversed mid-draft:** first pass avoided explicitly naming mother/father in the anchor labels, reasoning that naming implies ranking. Dan corrected this — unmarked language defaults to maternal by the design system's own logic, so avoiding names reads as omission, not neutrality, to a father skimming the page. Anchors now explicitly name Mothers / Fathers & partners / On your own, equally weighted; a general anchor may sit alongside but doesn't substitute for naming.
- **Main body updated from "woven-in moments" to explicit subheadings:** once anchors name specific audiences, they need somewhere legible to land — an anchor dropping into an undifferentiated paragraph doesn't visibly do its job. Added a real subheading per named audience. Kept deliberately un-boxed/un-carded (same visual treatment as any other subheading) so the page still reads as one continuous piece rather than sliding into the fully-split "option 2" the original three-way choice was trying to avoid.
- **Cross-link block heading is now marked as a working title**, not final copy — "now you're oriented" was workshop shorthand.
- **Per-subheading "learn more" links added** (Dan's addition) — each named audience gets its own natural exit point, not just one generic list at the page foot. Surfaced a real content gap in the process: the content schedule has an audience-tailored pamphlet for fathers/partners (*Fathers Grieve Too*) but nothing equivalent for mothers or solo parents specifically — flagged rather than papered over with a false-parity generic link.
