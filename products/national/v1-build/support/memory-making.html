Memory making

DRAFT rewrite — tone and examples under dual bereaved-parent review. The old national page mixed helpful ideas with insensitive suggestions; this page starts quieter.

When a baby dies, time together can feel impossibly short. Other parents have found that creating memories — if and when it feels right — can matter for years afterwards. There is no list you must complete, and no wrong choice.

We encourage you to go gently. You may feel differently tomorrow. You can change your mind.

In hospital or soon after birth

  • Photographs — including details (hands, feet, profile) if that feels right; some hospitals work with volunteer photographers such as Heartfelt
  • Hand and foot prints — inkless kits are available through many hospitals and Sands groups
  • Casting of hands or feet where a service is available
  • Keeping a blanket, hat, or name band; being clear with staff what stays with you and what goes with baby
  • A lock of hair, cot card, or other small keepsakes in a memory box

Support packs often include materials that help with this. Support resources · Certificate of Life

Later, when you're ready

  • Jewellery, candles, gardens, or other personal remembrances
  • Writing, photographs, or a book of condolences
  • Free or donated services offered to bereaved families in New Zealand — see Free memorial services

You don't have to do this alone

Hospital staff, your midwife, funeral directors, and Sands volunteers can help you think through options without pressure. Find a local group or contact us.
