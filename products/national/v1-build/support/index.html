How we can support you

Grief can feel deeply isolating, but you do not have to carry it alone. Sands offers compassionate, non-judgemental support in different ways. You can connect with a local group, talk with a bereaved-parent volunteer, join an online chat group, explore memory-making, or read resources in your own time.

Begin wherever feels right for you. There is no expectation to talk, share or do more than you feel ready for.

Talk to us

Depending on the region, Sands volunteers may offer support by phone, email, message or through local meetings. Many of our volunteers are bereaved parents who understand something of the grief that follows the death of a baby.

We are not counsellors. We are here to listen, acknowledge your baby and offer compassionate peer support.

If there is no active group in your region, or you are unsure where to begin, contact Sands New Zealand and we will help you find the support available to you.

If you need to talk to someone right now

Sands is not a crisis service, and messages are not monitored continuously. The following services are available throughout Aotearoa.

  • 1737, Need to Talk? — Call or text 1737 for free, confidential support from a trained counsellor, 24 hours a day.
  • Healthline — Call 0800 611 116 for free health advice, 24 hours a day. Healthline can also help connect you with your regional mental health crisis team.
  • Lifeline — Call 0800 543 354 or text HELP to 4357 for free, confidential emotional support.
  • Regional mental health crisis teams — Find the crisis team for your region.

If you or someone else is in immediate danger, call 111 or go to the nearest hospital emergency department.

Support group meetings

Connect in person with a local group

Some Sands groups hold regular meetings where bereaved parents and whānau can connect with others who understand. You are welcome to talk about your baby and your grief, or simply listen and support others. There is no pressure to share.

Meeting times, locations and frequency vary, and not every group offers in-person meetings. Contact your nearest group to find out what is currently available.

Find your local group and meeting details

Online support meetings

Sands New Zealand facilitates regular online support meetings through Zoom, allowing bereaved parents and whānau throughout Aotearoa to connect from their own homes.

You are welcome to share your experiences, talk about your baby or simply listen. There is no pressure to speak.

Meetings are generally held monthly, with a break or changes to the schedule during late December and early to mid-January. All meeting times are New Zealand time.

Online, any time

Sands New Zealand hosts private Facebook groups where bereaved parents and whānau across Aotearoa can share experiences, ask questions and connect with others who understand. You can read or post whenever it feels right for you. The pages are moderated by Sands NZ.

To join, request access, answer a few membership questions and agree to the group guidelines. This helps protect members’ privacy and maintain compassionate, respectful spaces.

Certificate of Life

Every baby deserves to be recognised, no matter how briefly they were with us. A Sands Certificate of Life is a personal keepsake acknowledging your baby’s existence and the place they hold in your whānau.

Remembrance & events

Through the year Sands groups and the national network hold events to remember our babies — for any gestation, and any time since your loss. These include Baby Loss Awareness Week, Light Up Aotearoa, the Global Wave of Light, and the biennial national conference.

Other support

When you're ready to look further afield, these organisations may help too:

  • Miscarriage Support — support after miscarriage (regional availability varies).
  • Miscarriage Matters — information and community.
  • Skylight Trust — grief support for all ages. skylight.org.nz

Whetūrangitia

Whetūrangitia is a government resource that brings together parents' rights and practical, legal, financial, burial and coronial information in one place, developed jointly across six agencies. The name speaks of stars returning to the sky to join the ancestors.
