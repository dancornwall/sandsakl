Someone I love has lost a baby

When a baby dies, the grief can be far reaching and touch many family and friends. You may be trying to support someone you love while also grieving this baby in your own way. Both of these experiences are real, and both deserve care and acknowledgement.

How you can help

You do not need to find the perfect words or make the grief better. Often, the most helpful thing you can do is acknowledge the baby, listen without judgement and continue to be present.

You might:

  • use the baby's name, if one was given
  • listen without trying to offer explanations or solutions
  • avoid beginning sentences with “at least”
  • offer specific practical help, such as preparing a meal or caring for other tamariki
  • remember birthdays, due dates and anniversaries
  • continue checking in after the first few weeks and months

Let the bereaved parents guide you. Some may want to talk about their baby, while others may not feel ready. Both are okay.

Partners

You are a bereaved parent too. We are sorry for your loss and recognise your need for support and acknowledgement.

You may feel pressure to be strong, manage practical arrangements or focus on supporting your partner while your own grief goes unnoticed. We encourage you to take care of yourself and seek support when you need it.

Two people can love and grieve the same baby differently. You may have different emotions, needs or ways of coping, and these may change over time. This does not mean either of you loved your baby less or is grieving incorrectly.

Sands welcomes partners seeking support for themselves. Bereaved fathers can also connect with other fathers through our online Bereaved Dads Chat.

Grandparents

When a grandchild dies, grandparents can experience a double grief. You are grieving your mokopuna while also witnessing the pain of your own child.

Your grief is real and deserves space. You can support the baby's parents by listening, acknowledging their baby and following their wishes, while also seeking support for yourself.

Sands offers online support specifically for bereaved grandparents.

Bereaved Grandparents Chat

Siblings and other tamariki

Tamariki grieve in ways shaped by their age and understanding. They may feel sad, angry, frightened or confused, and they may return to the same questions many times.

Use simple, honest language and reassure tamariki that the baby's death was not their fault. Where appropriate, invite them to take part in creating memories or farewelling their sibling, while allowing them to choose what feels comfortable.

Maintaining familiar routines and continuing to offer reassurance can help tamariki feel safe and supported. Your local Sands group may be able to help with resources for your tamariki or you may wish to contact Skylight Trust - a national not-for-profit helping tamariki and rangatahi aged 5 - 24 years navigate tough times and making support accessible to anyone who needs it. Visit - https://www.skylight.org.nz/

Friends and wider whānau

Friends and whānau may be grieving too. You are welcome to seek support from Sands for your own grief as well as guidance on supporting someone you love.

You do not have to carry this alone.
