Someone I love has lost a baby

⚠ DRAFT copy — orientation copy needs bereaved-parent review before it ships.

This page is for the people around a bereaved parent — partners, grandparents, whānau and friends. You want to help someone you love, and you may well be grieving this baby yourself. Both of those things are real.

If your own baby has died, you belong through the front door, as a parent — see When your baby dies. This page is for those supporting a bereaved parent.

Partners

You may be holding a great deal — your own grief, and the wish to support someone else in theirs. Grief rarely runs to the same timetable for two people, even when they have lost the same baby. That difference is normal, and it doesn't mean either of you is grieving wrongly.

Grandparents

When a grandchild dies, you carry a double load — your own grief for the baby, and the pain of watching your own child suffer. Your loss is real and often unseen. You are welcome to reach out to us too.

Brothers & sisters

Surviving children grieve in their own way, and often need help to understand what has happened. Grieving is the normal process for adjusting to the loss of someone we love — for them as much as for adults.
