My loss was a long time ago

⚠ DRAFT copy — orientation copy needs bereaved-parent review before it ships.

There is no expiry on grief, and no point at which it becomes too late to get in touch. Whether your baby died last year or thirty years ago, you are welcome here, and you don't need a reason to reach out now.

Grief doesn't end — because the love doesn't

You are not expected to have moved on. The relationship with your baby is a permanent one, and it is normal for the grief to stay with you, quietly, for the rest of your life. Some days it is barely there; other days it returns with full force. Neither is a step backwards.

Anniversaries, due dates and birthdays can reignite grief years on. A stillborn baby has two dates the world tends to forget — the due date that never came, and the day your baby was born. It is not strange to feel both.

The door is still open

Some people come to Sands for the first time decades after their loss. You are just as welcome as someone whose loss is recent. You might like to come to a meeting, read the newsletter, or simply talk to someone who understands.
