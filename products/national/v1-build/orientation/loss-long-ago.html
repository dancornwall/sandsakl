My baby died a long time ago

No matter how much time has passed, it is never too late to reach out. Whether your baby died last year or many decades ago, you are welcome here. You do not need a particular reason to seek support now.

Love and grief have no timeline

Grief does not follow a set path or timetable. It may change over the years, and there may be times when it feels close again. Birthdays, due dates, anniversaries, family milestones or unexpected reminders can bring memories and emotions to the surface.

Feeling grief again does not mean you are going backwards. Your baby remains part of your life, your whānau and your story.

If your loss was not acknowledged

Many years ago, parents were not always offered the understanding, choices or opportunities to create memories that may be available today. You may have been expected to remain silent, carry on or grieve alone. You may have few keepsakes or feel that your baby was never fully acknowledged.

Whatever happened at the time, your baby mattered and your grief matters.

The door is still open

Bereaved parents contact Sands months, years and sometimes decades after their baby has died. You are just as welcome as someone whose loss is recent.

You may wish to talk about your baby, connect with others, attend a Sands meeting or simply be heard by someone who understands. We are here whenever you feel ready.
