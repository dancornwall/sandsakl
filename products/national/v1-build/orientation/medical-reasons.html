My pregnancy ended for medical reasons

We are so sorry that you received a diagnosis that led to the ending of your pregnancy and the death of your baby. This is a profound loss, and you may be grieving while also trying to make sense of circumstances you never expected to face.

You may feel sadness, shock, anger, guilt, doubt, relief or many emotions at once. There is no right way to feel. Your baby matters, your experience matters and you deserve compassionate, non-judgemental support.

Receiving a diagnosis

Receiving an unexpected diagnosis during pregnancy can be devastating. It may be difficult to understand or remember medical information while you are in shock.

Take the time available to ask questions and gather the information you need. You may wish to:

  • ask your healthcare professionals to explain the diagnosis and your options again
  • request information in writing
  • write down your questions before appointments
  • have someone you trust attend appointments with you
  • ask how much time you have before a decision is needed
  • ask what to expect physically and emotionally
  • discuss opportunities to spend time with your baby and create memories

It is okay to ask the same question more than once or to say that you need more time to understand.

A deeply personal experience

Having to decide whether to end a wanted pregnancy following a medical diagnosis is a situation no parent should ever have to face. Your decision was shaped by the medical information you received, your circumstances, your values and your love for your baby. You do not have to explain or defend it to anyone.

You may hear terms such as termination for medical reasons, TFMR, or ending a wanted pregnancy. You can use whichever words feel right for you. It is also your choice what information you share with others. Some parents choose to explain their experience and their baby's diagnosis, others choose to say that their baby died in pregnancy.

After your pregnancy has ended

Your healthcare team can explain what to expect during your physical recovery, what follow-up care is available and who to contact if you have concerns. You may also be offered testing or a post mortem to help understand your baby's diagnosis.

You may wish to hold, name or dress your baby, take photographs, create keepsakes, or arrange a blessing or farewell ceremony. You may choose some of these things, all of them or none of them. These are invitations, not expectations.

Our support and information booklet, A little life, not a little loss, contains further information about receiving a diagnosis, making decisions, physical recovery, creating memories and saying goodbye.

Sands is here

Sands offers compassionate, non-judgemental support, regardless of the diagnosis you received or the decision you made. We can listen without judgement and connect you with bereaved parents who may have experienced something similar.

You do not have to carry this alone.
