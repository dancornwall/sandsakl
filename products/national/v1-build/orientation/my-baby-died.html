My baby has died

We are so sorry that your baby has died. Whether your baby died during pregnancy, at birth, as a newborn or in infancy, your baby matters and your grief matters.

The hours and days ahead may feel overwhelming. It can be difficult to take in information or know what to do next. There is no right way to feel, and your emotions may change from one moment or day to the next.

The information on this page can be read at your own pace. When you are ready, Sands is here with compassionate support from bereaved parents who have also experienced the death of a baby. You do not have to face this alone.

In the first hours and days

You may be asked to make choices you never expected to face. Ask your healthcare team to explain your options, tell you what needs attention now and what can wait, and give you time wherever possible. There is no rush. There are very few decisions that truly cannot wait. You may also wish to have someone you trust beside you to listen, take notes or simply be there.

Spending time with your baby

There is no right or wrong way to spend time with or farewell your baby. You may wish to hold, name or dress your baby, take photographs, create keepsakes, or arrange a blessing or ceremony. You may choose some of these things, all of them or none of them. These are invitations, not expectations. What matters is what feels right for you, your baby and your whānau.

Information for when you are ready

Our support and information booklet, A little life, not a little loss, offers gentle, practical guidance for bereaved parents and whānau. It includes information about:

  • physical recovery and follow-up care
  • creating memories and keepsakes
  • post mortem examinations
  • taking your baby home and transporting them
  • birth and death registration
  • funerals, burial, cremation and farewell ceremonies
  • financial support and leave entitlements
  • supporting partners, grandparents and tamariki

You do not need to read it all at once. Begin with what feels helpful and return to it whenever you are ready.

Sands is here

Sands offers compassionate, non-judgemental support from bereaved parents who understand the grief that follows the death of a baby. When you are ready, you can connect with a local Sands group or access support online.
