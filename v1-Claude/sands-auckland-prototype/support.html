If your baby has just died

My baby died Draft

There is no need to make hurried decisions. You may change your mind about what you decide today, and tomorrow you may feel very differently. Please do not feel any need to rush.

When you are ready, a Sands volunteer can talk with you — by phone or by email. Every one of our volunteers is a bereaved parent. We are not counsellors, and we will not give you advice. We will listen.

If you cannot make the first call yourself, please ask a family member or friend to get in touch, and we will contact you.

0508 SANDSA (726 372)  ·  info@sandsauckland.org.nz

My loss was a long time ago Draft

Grief does not have an expiry date, and there is no point at which it becomes too late to talk to us. Some of the people at our meetings lost their babies decades ago. Some have never spoken about it before. You have not missed your chance, and you do not need a reason to get in touch now.

Anniversaries, due dates and birthdays can bring grief back sharply, years later. So can a new pregnancy in the family, or a chance remark. If this year is harder than last year, that is not a step backwards.

I'm pregnant again Draft

Pregnancy after loss is not the same as pregnancy. It can carry a fear that other people around you may not see or understand, and joy and dread can sit side by side for months.

Parents who are subsequently pregnant are welcome to contact us, and welcome at our meetings.

Someone I love has lost a baby Draft

You may be a partner, a grandparent, a sibling, a friend or a colleague. You may be grieving yourself while also trying to hold someone else up.

Fathers and partners

The expectation of some family members, friends and a large proportion of society can often dictate the way you live with this loss and what you do with your grief. Sands NZ runs a closed Bereaved Dads Chat group, and the Sands pamphlet Fathers Grieve Too was written for you. You are a bereaved parent, not a supporter of one.

Grandparents

When a grandchild dies, you have to cope with a double load — your own grief for the baby and the suffering of your own child. Sands NZ runs a closed Bereaved Grandparents Chat group, and the pamphlet The Loss of a Grandchild may help.

Brothers and sisters

As you grieve for your baby who has died, you may also need to deal with the emotions and reactions of your other children. You are all part of the same family and have all lost a precious family member. Grieving is the normal process for adjusting to the loss of someone we love. The pamphlet When a Baby Brother or Sister Dies is written for parents supporting siblings.

Whatever happened

We support families through any kind of loss Draft

Sands Auckland Central supports parents and families who have experienced the death of a baby at any gestation, and under any circumstance — including miscarriage, ectopic pregnancy, stillbirth, termination for medical reasons, newborn death, and the death of a baby in infancy.

If your loss is not named here, it still counts, and you are still welcome to call.

How we help

Phone and email support

Unable to attend a support meeting? Would you like to talk with someone one-on-one? You can chat with a Sands volunteer by calling 0508 SANDSA (726 372).

If we are unable to answer your call, please leave a message and we will return your call within 24 hours.

Support group meeting

When
First Thursday of each month, 7:15 – 8:45 pm
Months
February to December — no meeting in January
Where
Mount Albert Community and Leisure Centre (formerly The Y Mt Albert)
773 New North Rd, Mt Albert
Room
The Garlick Room — turn left at the reception desk

Monthly support meetings allow for the expression of loss and grief in a safe, non-judgmental and caring environment. They are available to parents and families to talk, if they wish, about their babies who have died. Parents who share similar experiences can often listen, acknowledge and accept feelings that others may find hard to comprehend. These meetings are facilitated by one of our support volunteers.

There are no childcare facilities available — these meetings are for adults only.

Please check our Facebook page for updates. In rare circumstances meetings may need to be cancelled due to illness or unforeseen circumstances.

Online support

Sands New Zealand offers several closed Facebook groups for online chat support. These can be a good way to connect with other grieving parents across New Zealand, and to ask questions at any hour.

To join you will need to request access, answer a few questions and agree to the group guidelines set by Sands NZ. These are in place so the groups stay a safe and respectful place to grieve and share. The groups are moderated by Sands NZ board members.

Sands support packs

We aim to offer support items — information packs, inkless kits and special memory booklets — to parents through Auckland City, North Shore and Waitakere hospitals. We can also offer packs or memory booklets directly to families, or through independent LMCs.

These items can be an important part of the memory building process, as there is often little time available to spend with our babies. Please contact us if you would like more information.

The following pamphlets, produced by Sands New Zealand, are provided in the support pack.

The Next Few Days

When a baby dies, it is so hard to think clearly, yet you are faced with many difficult decisions. The next few days are a chance for you to build memories of your baby. These are ideas shared by other bereaved parents who want you to know that you are not alone.

Your Baby's Funeral
